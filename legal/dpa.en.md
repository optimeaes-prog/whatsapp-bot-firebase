## Recitals

A. Processor provides Controller with the Proplead platform, an AI-powered CRM designed for real estate agencies in Spain that enables management of WhatsApp conversations, automated lead qualification, AI-generated summaries, and related features (the “**Platform**”).

B. In order to provide the Platform, Processor processes personal data of Controller’s leads and contacts. This Data Processing Agreement (“**DPA**”) governs such processing in accordance with Article 28 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council (“**GDPR**”), applicable to Controller as an entity processing data of individuals located in the European Economic Area, and the UK General Data Protection Regulation (“**UK GDPR**”), applicable to Processor as a UK-established entity.

C. This DPA forms an integral part of the Proplead Terms of Service (the “**Main Agreement**”). In the event of conflict in relation to data protection matters, this DPA prevails.

## Clause 1. Definitions

For the purposes of this DPA, the following terms have the meanings indicated:

1.1 **”Personal Data”**: any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person as defined in Article 4(1) GDPR.

1.2 **”Processing”**: any operation or set of operations performed on personal data as defined in Article 4(2) GDPR.

1.3 **”Controller”**: the natural or legal person that determines the purposes and means of processing (Article 4(7) GDPR). For the purposes of this DPA, the Controller is the Customer.

1.4 **”Processor”**: the natural or legal person that processes personal data on behalf of the Controller (Article 4(8) GDPR). For the purposes of this DPA, the Processor is Talmate Limited.

1.5 **”Sub-processor”**: any third party engaged by Processor to carry out specific processing activities on behalf of Controller.

1.6 **”Controller Personal Data”**: the personal data that Processor processes on behalf of Controller under this DPA.

1.7 **”Personal Data Breach”**: a breach of security leading to the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorised disclosure of, or access to, personal data, as defined in Article 4(12) GDPR.

1.8 **”Instructions”**: Controller’s documented instructions regarding how Processor must process Controller Personal Data, including those set out in the Main Agreement, this DPA, and the configuration settings Controller applies within the Platform.

1.9 **”Data Subject”**: the natural person whose personal data is being processed.

1.10 **”SCCs”**: the Standard Contractual Clauses adopted by the European Commission under Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/914 of 4 June 2021.

1.11 **”IDTA”**: the International Data Transfer Agreement approved by the UK Information Commissioner’s Office (ICO).

1.12 **”TOMs”**: technical and organisational security measures.

1.13 **”Data Protection Law”**: the GDPR, the UK GDPR, applicable national implementing legislation (including Organic Law 3/2018 on Data Protection and Guarantee of Digital Rights in Spain), and any other applicable data protection legislation in force during the term of this DPA.

## Clause 8. International Transfers of Personal Data

8.1 **General principle.** Processor shall implement appropriate safeguards as required by Chapter V GDPR and Chapter V UK GDPR before transferring Controller Personal Data to a third country or international organisation.

8.2 **Sub-processors in the United States.** Processor’s current sub-processors are established in the United States of America (USA), which is a third country for both the European Union and the United Kingdom. Transfers to such sub-processors are covered by the following mechanisms:

   a) **EU/EEA → UK → USA flow:** Transfers of data relating to individuals located in the EEA (including Spain) are made on the basis of the **Standard Contractual Clauses** adopted by the European Commission (Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/914), under Module 2 (controller to processor) or Module 3 (processor to processor), as applicable to each specific transfer, together with any supplementary measures required by the applicable transfer impact assessment.

   b) **UK → USA flow:** Transfers of data processed under the UK GDPR are made on the basis of the **International Data Transfer Agreement (IDTA)** approved by the ICO, or the **UK Addendum** to the EU SCCs (also approved by the ICO), as applicable to each sub-processor.

8.3 **Dual framework (EU + UK).** As Talmate is established in the United Kingdom and processes data of EEA/EU residents (in particular, in Spain), Processor shall implement both mechanisms (SCCs and IDTA/UK Addendum) where both are simultaneously applicable, ensuring coverage under both jurisdictions.

8.4 **Supplementary measures.** In addition to the contractual mechanisms above, Processor shall implement technical and organisational supplementary measures to ensure a level of protection substantially equivalent to that guaranteed by the GDPR, including in particular: (i) encryption in transit using TLS 1.2 or higher; (ii) use of `store: false` in OpenAI API calls to limit data retention by the model; (iii) pseudonymisation where technically feasible; (iv) periodic review of sub-processors’ privacy policies and DPAs.

8.5 **Obligation to inform.** Processor shall inform Controller of any regulatory changes in the destination country of a transfer that may affect existing safeguards, and shall implement any alternative measures required.

## Clause 10. Personal Data Breaches

10.1 **Notification to Controller.** Processor, upon becoming aware of a Personal Data Breach affecting Controller Personal Data, shall notify Controller **without undue delay and, in any event, within a maximum of forty-eight (48) hours** of becoming aware of the breach, regardless of whether it is likely to result in a risk to the rights and freedoms of Data Subjects.

10.2 **Content of notification.** The notification shall include, to the extent Processor has the information available at the time of notification, at least:

   a) a description of the nature of the Personal Data Breach, including, where possible, the categories and approximate number of Data Subjects concerned and the categories and approximate number of personal data records concerned;

   b) the name and contact details of the data protection officer or other point of contact where more information can be obtained;

   c) a description of the likely consequences of the Personal Data Breach;

   d) a description of the measures taken or proposed to address the Personal Data Breach, including, where appropriate, measures to mitigate its possible adverse effects.

10.3 **Phased notification.** Where Processor is unable to provide all the information referred to in clause 10.2 at the same time, it may provide this information in phases without undue further delay.

10.4 **Cooperation.** Processor shall cooperate with Controller and provide all reasonable assistance necessary to enable Controller to fulfil its notification obligations to the supervisory authority (in Spain, the AEPD) within the seventy-two (72) hour deadline under Article 33 GDPR, and, where applicable, to communicate with Data Subjects under Article 34 GDPR.

10.5 **Incident management.** Processor shall promptly take reasonable measures to identify, contain and remediate the Personal Data Breach, and to prevent its recurrence.

10.6 **Record of incidents.** Processor shall maintain an internal record of Personal Data Breaches in accordance with Article 33(5) GDPR.

## Clause 12. Audits and Inspections

12.1 **Audit right.** Processor shall make available to Controller all information necessary to demonstrate compliance with this DPA and the obligations in Article 28 GDPR, and shall allow for and contribute to audits and inspections.

12.2 **Frequency.** Controller may exercise its audit right, as a general rule, **no more than once (1) per calendar year**, except in cases of a material security incident, reasonable grounds for suspecting non-compliance, or a requirement from a competent supervisory authority.

12.3 **Advance notice.** Controller shall notify Processor of its intention to conduct an audit with at least **twenty (20) business days’** prior notice, except in duly justified urgent situations.

12.4 **Conditions.** Audits shall be conducted:

   a) during Processor’s normal business hours;

   b) in a manner that causes the minimum disruption to Processor’s operations;

   c) by Controller or a duly qualified independent external auditor that has entered into a confidentiality agreement with Processor;

   d) without access to other customers’ data or to information subject to trade secrecy.

12.5 **Documentary alternative.** Processor may reasonably satisfy Controller’s audit right by providing third-party audit certificates (e.g., ISO/IEC 27001, SOC 2) or recent audit reports issued by independent auditors, in lieu of an on-site audit, where the information provided is sufficiently representative of Processor’s compliance.

## ANNEX 1 — Processing Details

### A. Subject matter of processing

Provision to Controller of the Proplead platform, an AI-powered CRM that enables real estate agencies in Spain to manage communications with their leads via WhatsApp, automatically qualify leads using generative AI, generate lead summaries and profiles, manage conversation workflows, and track the status of each lead throughout the sales or rental process.

### B. Duration of processing

Processing shall extend throughout the entire duration of Controller’s subscription or use of the Platform, and during the data return or deletion period set out in Clause 13 of this DPA. In no case shall processing extend beyond the minimum retention period required by applicable law.

### C. Nature of processing

Processing activities comprise the following operations on Controller Personal Data:

- Receiving inbound WhatsApp messages (via Twilio webhooks) and storing them in the database.
- Sending outbound WhatsApp messages via the Twilio API.
- Storing conversation history and lead state in Firestore (Google Cloud).
- Running automated lead qualification workflows via OpenAI API calls (with `store: false`), including generating qualification questions and lead summaries.
- Supporting manual messaging by the agency’s agents through the Platform interface.
- Tagging, categorising and managing lead statuses within the Platform.
- Managing voice calls and recordings (where the voice module is enabled), via Twilio and/or Vapi AI.
- Storing and processing billing and subscription data via Stripe.
- Sending service-related email notifications.
- Logging and monitoring system operations (security and operational logs).

### D. Purpose of processing

Processing is carried out for the following purposes:

1. Provision of the Platform’s features to Controller, including lead conversation management, automated qualification and summary generation.
2. Platform maintenance, technical support, security and improvement.
3. Compliance with legal obligations applicable to Processor.

Processor does not process Controller Personal Data for its own purposes, commercial interests or direct marketing.

### E. Categories of Data Subjects

- Natural persons (leads or contacts) who have shown interest in properties managed by Controller and who communicate with Controller via WhatsApp or other channels integrated in the Platform.
- Natural persons who contact Controller via WhatsApp or telephone calls in connection with real estate services.
- Employees or contractors of Controller who use the Platform (users of Controller’s account).

### F. Categories of Personal Data processed

Personal data processed in the context of the Platform may include:

**Identifiers and contact data:**
- Phone number (primary lead identifier on WhatsApp)
- Chat identifiers (WhatsApp chat IDs)
- Call identifiers (call IDs)
- Timestamps (message and event timestamps)

**Communications content:**
- WhatsApp message text (inbound and outbound messages)
- Full conversation history

**Lead attributes (data provided by the lead or inferred by AI):**
- Lead’s name
- Household composition (number of household members, presence of children, pets)
- Available budget (numeric figure)
- Type of property sought (residential, commercial, office, etc.)
- Preferred location
- Preferred payment method
- Availability for property viewings
- Agent notes and observations
- Lead qualification status (label, position in the sales funnel)

**Voice and call data (where the voice module is enabled):**
- Call metadata (duration, date, time, originating and destination numbers)
- Call recordings and/or URLs for accessing recordings
- Call transcripts (where automatic transcription is enabled)

**Platform user data (Controller’s employees):**
- Name, email address, role in the organisation
- Access data and activity log within the Platform

### G. Special categories of data

Processing of special categories of personal data under Article 9 GDPR (health data, racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious or philosophical beliefs, trade union membership, genetic or biometric data, or data concerning sexual life or orientation) or data relating to criminal convictions and offences (Article 10 GDPR) is not intended.

Controller must avoid inputting or sharing such data with Processor. If, due to Controller’s specific operational needs, processing of such data is strictly necessary, Controller must notify Processor in advance and agree on additional appropriate measures.

### H. Processing operations (technical flow description)

Indicative technical flow of the Platform:

1. **Event reception:** Inbound WhatsApp messages or voice calls are received by Cloud Functions (Google Firebase) via Twilio webhooks (or Vapi AI for voice).
2. **Storage:** Messages and metadata are stored in Firestore (Google Cloud). The lead record is created or updated with the relevant attributes.
3. **Queueing:** A task is created in Google Cloud Tasks for message processing.
4. **AI processing:** The corresponding Cloud Function sends the relevant conversation history to the OpenAI API (with `store: false`) and receives the generated response or lead summary.
5. **Response:** The generated response is sent to the lead via the Twilio API (WhatsApp) or Vapi AI (voice).
6. **State update:** Lead attributes and qualification status are updated in Firestore and displayed to the agent in the Platform interface.
7. **Notifications:** Processor may send service notifications by email to Controller’s users.

## ANNEX 2 — Technical and Organisational Measures (TOMs)

Processor implements the following technical and organisational measures to ensure the security of Controller Personal Data:

### 1. Access management and authentication

- **Role-based access control (RBAC):** Access to Processor’s systems and customer data is restricted on a least-privilege basis. Only personnel who require access for their duties may access Controller Personal Data.
- **Multi-factor authentication (MFA):** Multi-factor authentication is required for access to production systems, cloud administration consoles and development tools.
- **Password management:** Robust password policies are applied, and corporate password managers are used.
- **Periodic access reviews:** Processor periodically reviews its personnel’s access permissions and revokes unnecessary access.
- **Environment separation:** Development, testing and production environments are logically separated.

### 2. Encryption

- **Encryption in transit:** All communications between Processor’s systems, sub-processors and Platform users use TLS 1.2 or higher (HTTPS).
- **Encryption at rest:** Data stored in Firebase/Firestore and Google Cloud Storage is encrypted at rest using Google Cloud’s standard measures (AES-256).
- **Secure secrets management:** API keys, tokens and other secrets are managed via Google Cloud Secret Manager and secure environment variables, and are never included in source code.

### 3. Logging and monitoring

- **Operational logs:** Processor maintains logs of key system operations (inbound webhooks, external API calls, data updates) via Google Cloud Logging.
- **Security monitoring:** Security alerts from infrastructure providers (Google Cloud, Firebase) are monitored and reviewed periodically.
- **Log integrity:** Logs are retained in systems with independent access controls to prevent tampering.

### 4. Vulnerability management and updates

- **Dependency updates:** Processor applies security updates to software dependencies on a regular basis.
- **Code review:** Platform code is subject to review before deployment to production.
- **Security testing:** Processor conducts periodic security reviews of the code and infrastructure.

### 5. Incident response

- **Incident response procedure:** Processor maintains a documented procedure for detecting, analysing, containing, eliminating and recovering from security breaches.
- **Internal reporting channel:** An internal security incident reporting channel exists for Processor’s personnel.
- **Notification to Controller:** Processor shall notify Controller of security breaches as set out in Clause 10 of this DPA.
- **Lessons learned:** Processor documents incidents and the corrective measures taken to prevent recurrence.

### 6. Business continuity and disaster recovery

- **Backups:** Firestore performs automatic backups managed by Google Cloud, with disaster recovery capability.
- **High availability:** The Platform is deployed on Firebase/Google Cloud infrastructure, which provides high availability and geographic redundancy.
- **Continuity plans:** Processor maintains business continuity plans for the Platform’s critical services.

### 7. Physical security

Processor’s infrastructure is hosted in Google Cloud data centres, which have certified physical security measures (ISO/IEC 27001, SOC 2). Processor does not manage its own physical infrastructure for processing Controller Personal Data.

### 8. Personnel training and awareness

- Processor’s personnel with access to Controller Personal Data receive periodic training on data protection and information security.
- All employees and contractors with access to data are subject to contractual confidentiality obligations.

### 9. AI-specific measures

- OpenAI API calls are made with the `store: false` parameter to prevent conversation data from being stored by OpenAI beyond the context of the call.
- Prompts sent to OpenAI include only the minimum information necessary for the qualification or summary function.
- Processor periodically reviews OpenAI’s data use policies and will update measures if those policies change.

### 10. Firestore security rules

- Firebase Firestore security rules prevent unauthorised cross-customer access to any customer’s data (multi-tenant isolation).
- Access to each organisation’s (Controller’s) data is restricted to authenticated users of that organisation.
